Seller counts are one of the most quoted marketplace metrics and one of the easiest to misread. A category can gain or lose real competitive pressure long before the count moves.

## Counts stabilise, behaviour does not

Platforms rarely delete inactive listings. A shop that stopped selling two years ago may still be counted as registered. That means "registered sellers" is a stock number that only grows, while "active sellers" depends entirely on the activity window someone chose.

A 30-day active definition and a 90-day active definition can differ by a factor of two on the same platform.

## Entry and exit are slow

Opening a marketplace store takes days, but building enough listings and reviews to compete takes months. When demand shifts, the visible response is usually existing sellers changing assortment and price, not new sellers appearing. The count catches up later.

That makes the count a poor early signal and a reasonable confirmation signal.

## What to watch instead

- Listing growth in the specific category, not the whole platform
- Share of listings held by the top 1% of shops
- Average price movement within a category
- Review velocity per listing, which tracks real demand

Use seller counts to describe the shape of a market, not to call a turn in it.

## Definition matters more than the number

Before comparing two seller counts, confirm:

1. The activity window (30, 90 or 365 days)
2. Whether the count is by shop or by listing
3. Whether cross-border sellers are included
4. Whether the market is defined by seller location or buyer location

Two counts that disagree are usually measuring two different things rather than contradicting each other.
